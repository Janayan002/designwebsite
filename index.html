Tack

A canvas for visual research where every image remembers where it came from

I designed and built Tack to replace a research process of 50+ open tabs, copied images and separately pasted links. You search the web from a panel beside your board, drop images, links and notes onto the canvas, and Tack keeps the source and date of every image automatically.

Role
Design and development, solo
Year
2026
Tools
Figma, Next.js, Vercel, Claude API
Visit live site ↗

Challenge

When research is visual, an image and its source end up in two different places: the image on a board, the link in a browser tab. Only memory connects them, and closing one tab can break a citation for good.

Solution

A board where the source travels with the image. Search sits beside the canvas, and everything you add keeps where and when you found it.

Boards save in the browser from the first visit; signing in moves them to an account. A browser extension and an AI research assistant extend the same workflow.

Constraints

I built Tack alone, so two technical limits shaped the design as much as user needs did. AI cost: every call costs money, so AI is an optional layer on top of the core workflow, not the core itself. Storage: images take up space quickly, so boards save in the browser first and accounts are for people who need their boards to last.

The starting point

For an artist research assignment, I had to collect work by 10–20 artists who inspire me. By the end I had more than 50 tabs open, jumping between Instagram, portfolio sites and online journals. Collecting the images was easy. Keeping track of where each one came from was the tedious part.

My workflow used three tools, and each one failed in a different way:

ToolHow I used itWhere it broke
FigmaBoard for laying out imagesCopy each image from a tab, then copy its URL separately for the citation
Google SlidesPresentation-style research deckSame double copy-paste; closing a tab by accident lost the link for good
PinterestSaving referencesOnly images already on Pinterest; if the uploader deletes a pin, the image is gone

The real problem wasn’t layout. An image and its source were stored in two different places, and only my memory and my open tabs connected them.

Tack starts from the idea that the two should never be separated.

Who it’s for

Tack started as a tool for myself, but the same problem shows up for anyone whose research is visual: art and design students, fashion, architecture, art history and marketing. Early conversations suggested the idea works beyond my own use:

PersonFieldWhat they’d use it for
Friend AFashionBuilding mood boards
Friend BDesignCollecting type and image inspiration
Friend COutside creative fieldsSaving images for fun, like animation characters he likes

The third response stood out: someone outside creative fields saw a use for it right away, which pointed to a broader audience than I had planned for.

Key decisions

Four decisions shaped Tack. Each time I chose against the obvious default, which was usually “do what Figma does” or “let the AI do it.”

  1. 01Pages instead of layers

    Default
    A Figma-style layers panel on the left.
    Chose
    Pages, with several visible on the same screen.
    Why
    Creatives find inspiration by spotting connections between different subjects. Layers organise one board; pages let you compare several research threads side by side.

  2. 02Google-style search instead of an AI chat

    Default
    Ask an AI assistant and let it return results.
    Chose
    A familiar search panel beside the board. Links open in a preview next to the canvas, or in the browser.
    Why
    Search is more hands-on: you decide which sites to visit and where your images come from. For research that has to be cited, that control matters more than speed. The AI assistant supports search; it doesn’t replace it.

  3. 03A sketchbook, not a design tool

    Before
    A dark-mode interface that looked like Figma.
    Chose
    A light white-and-beige “sketchbook” style with more varied icons.
    Why
    Artists research in sketchbooks, not in design software. A physical-tool feel makes Tack a place to think and collect, not a place to produce final work.

  4. 04Save in the browser first, account optional

    Default
    Require sign-up before you can make a board.
    Chose
    Boards save in the browser right away; signing in moves them to an account.
    Why
    Someone doing a one-time project can start with no setup. People who come back have a reason to sign in.

Deep dive: source tracking

Every image on a Tack board keeps its source and the date it was found. It’s the feature that answers “why not just use Pinterest or Figma?”

Problem

In my old workflow, each image needed two copy-pastes: the image, then its URL. The link lived only in an open tab, so closing that tab could break the citation for good. On Pinterest, the image itself could disappear if the uploader deleted it.

Where sources are hard to keep

  • Image links that expire. Instagram and Facebook image links stop working after a few days, so for those sites the Tack Clipper extension saves a copy of the picture itself, not just the link.
  • Pages where right-click fails. On Instagram, right-click doesn’t reach the picture. A Pick images mode lets you choose pictures straight from the page.
  • Saving with no board open. Clipped items wait in a queue, and the extension icon shows how many, until a board is opened.

Process

Tack began as Research Board, a tool that opened on a frame-size picker and was built around searching with AI. Over the versions, the name, the colour scheme and the centre of the product all changed, from an AI that finds things for you to a board where you do the finding and Tack keeps track.

Outcome and next steps

Tack is live at tack-eta.vercel.app, with boards, folders, web search, source tracking, an AI assistant and a browser extension.
